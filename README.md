# India CPI Inflation Analysis (2013–2023)

## Project Overview
This project explores India's Consumer Price Index (CPI) data to understand how price levels vary across sectors, categories, and time. The Excel workbook includes cleaned/unpivoted data, year-over-year CPI analysis, food-price analysis, pre/post-COVID comparisons, and correlation analysis between Fuel & Light and selected CPI categories.

## Objectives
- Explore CPI patterns across Rural, Urban, and Rural+Urban sectors.
- Review year-over-year CPI movement and identify periods of stronger inflation pressure.
- Compare food-category price indices and their movement over time.
- Compare selected CPI categories before and after COVID-19.
- Assess relationships between Fuel & Light and other CPI components using correlation.

## Tools
- Microsoft Excel
- PivotTables / PivotCharts
- Data cleaning and unpivoting
- Descriptive analysis and correlation

## Workbook Guide
Open `India_CPI_Inflation.xlsx` to explore the analysis. The workbook includes:
- **Clean Data unpivoted** — long-format dataset with Sector, Year, Month, Attribute, Value, and Classified As fields.
- **1.Category Contribution** — distribution of observations across broader categories.
- **2.Y-O-Y CPI Inflation** — annual CPI trend by sector.
- **3.Food price analysis / 3.2 Food Price Analysis** — food-category index comparisons.
- **4.Pre and Post COVID CPI / 4.1 pre - post COVID (Month-Wise)** — comparisons across selected categories around the COVID period.
- **5. Co-relation with oil prices** and related sheets — correlations between Fuel & Light and other CPI components.

## Key Findings
- Food represents approximately **44.4%** of the classified observations in the workbook, making it an important category to monitor.
- The workbook's annual analysis shows overall CPI inflation pressure peaking around **2022 (about 6.6%)**, followed by easing to approximately **3.2% in 2023**.
- Food-price analysis highlights vegetables as a comparatively volatile category, while milk and pulses show more consistent upward movement in many periods.
- Fuel & Light has a **positive correlation of about 0.66** with Transport & Communication in the analysed series.
- Other correlations shown in the workbook include approximately **0.40** with Health, **0.42** with Clothing & Footwear, **0.31** with Household Goods & Services, **0.23** with Education, and **-0.20** with Food & Beverages.

## Notes and Limitations
- Findings reflect the data and calculations contained in this workbook; they should not be treated as proof of causation.
- Correlation indicates association, not causation.
- Before publishing or using this project for formal research, verify the original data source, definitions, date coverage, and any calculations against the authoritative CPI source.
